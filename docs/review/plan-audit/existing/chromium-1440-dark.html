Count the spaces

Selinus, Temple C beside Vitruvius’s count.

The selected reading predicts 11 flank columns. The catalogue records 17. Six columns enclose five spaces. Doubling five spaces gives ten, with eleven columns at their ends; doubling six columns gives twelve, with eleven spaces.

These are count diagrams with equal spacing, not measured plans. Matching a count cannot show that a builder followed Vitruvius. The buildings mostly precede his first-century BCE treatise. This curated catalogue is not a census.

Vitruvius III.4.3

ita columnae in peripteris conlocentur, uti, quot intercolumnia sunt in fronte, totidem bis intercolumnia fiant in lateribus; ita enim erit duplex longitudo operis ad latitudinem. namque qui columnarum duplicationes fecerunt, erravisse videntur, quod unum intercolumnium in longitudine plus quam oporteat procurrere videtur.

In peripteral temples set the columns so that the flanks have twice as many intercolumniations as the front; then the length of the work will be double its width. Those who doubled the columns seem to have erred, because the length then appears to run on by one intercolumniation more than it should.

Latin: Krohn (1912), p. 69; new English translation, not independently reviewed. Printed witness.
