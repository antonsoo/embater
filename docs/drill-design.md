# A vocabulary exercise on an architectural plate

The audience is English-speaking learners meeting source-language names through
visible parts of buildings. Keep the current architectural engraving: cool marble
`#eceee9`, sheet `#f8f9f5`, graphite `#1f2328`, Egyptian blue `#1f4e9c`, minium
`#b3321f`. GFS Didot names the exercise; EB Garamond carries words and notes.
The existing dark palette remains available.

The plate is the memorable element. Focus or hover over a numbered part control
to inspect its geometry; activate the control to answer. Numbers identify controls,
not archaeological features. A separate unscored study state names those controls
in English and opens the existing word notes. This gives a reading route through
the visual material without claiming a blind user can recognize an anonymous SVG.

    deck / language / study or question
    +--------------------+-------------------+
    | architectural      | source word or    |
    | plate              | answer choices    |
    | inspected geometry | explanation       |
    +--------------------+-------------------+
    numbered part controls / progress for this language and direction

On a phone the question precedes the plate, and the compact part controls follow
the plate. The feedback follows the answer controls, so a wrong answer does not
throw the learner back above the drawing. Avoid a new HUD, badges or a separate
visual skin: the drawing and selective blue ink already belong to this subject.

An initially considered permanent numbered overlay would obscure small mouldings.
Native buttons which preview one shape at a time give the same keyboard route
without covering the engraving. The study list provides the part names and the
word card; practice hides those names until a response. Focus never grades.
