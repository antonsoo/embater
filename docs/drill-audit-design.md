# Audit direction

The existing architectural plate remains the main object: cool marble #eceee9,
sheet #f8f9f5, graphite #1f2328, blue #1f4e9c, minium #b3321f; GFS Didot and
EB Garamond retain their current roles. English-speaking learners need to see
small mouldings and inspect the historical text that names or sizes them.

The audit found small selections whose color changes only a few dozen phone
pixels. A second permanent plate would make an already long phone lesson longer.
Instead, a Whole plate / Local detail control reuses the same figure, geometry
and part buttons. Blue continues to mark the selected shape. The local crop may
show only a portion of a long feature and must say so. Zoom and pan never grade.
The whole plate remains one action away. Large features start with a wider
context crop so a walk or a roof does not become an unexplained flat blue band. Do not invent extra carving detail.

    [Whole plate] [Local detail]     [less] [more] [recenter]
    +---------------------------------------------------+
    | same SVG geometry, enlarged around selected part  |
    +---------------------------------------------------+
    focus a numbered part / answer or study it

The printed source opens beneath its word card. Its citation, complete page
image, enlargement and recovery controls belong together. Loading or replacing
a page cannot erase a question or change practice boxes. Source failures stay
visible; an unverified image cannot masquerade as the checked page. Keep the
editor's differing readings visible alongside the excerpt. The full printed
apparatus is evidence, not a decorative historical texture.

An illustrated study, if retained, should carry the actual selected shape and
source pages with their fonts and licenses. It must open without scripts or
remote requests. Progress and quiz outcomes are separate from that reading.
